# Graven status

Taken on 13 November 2026, on branch `ui-dev`. This list comes from the commit history, the code layout and recent work. Not every feature was re-checked in the app for it.

Graven has about 32,000 lines of Zig and 209 tests. It has 127 commits since 4 November 2026, and 81 of them are on `ui-dev` and not yet on `main`.

**Applications** (the owner, 2026-11-20, adding tabletop gaming): keypads and keycaps; control panels; tabletop gaming: game boards, tiles, tokens and markers.

## What exists

### Legend engine (`lib/glyphic`, `lib/font`, `lib/geom`)

v1 (`lib/engine`) was deleted on 2026-11-16 (step 5 of the route onto v2); glyphic sets every legend. What it has, by area (its stages and steps are below, under "Planned: engine v2"):

- **Shaping and outlines:** HarfBuzz shaping; glyph outlines kept exact (lines, quadratics, cubics); cap height measured from the outlines.
- **Entry:** LaTeX as KaTeX takes it (the default) and a Typst-style syntax, both to the same objects; parsers never fail (partial input gives holes); spans per object and per field. A legend is text with math between dollars; an equation run is all math. Emphasis (`\textbf`, `\textit`, `\emph`), `\mathbf`/`\boldsymbol`, operator names, `\allowbreak`, soft hyphens. Element overrides in Graven's call form, `\set(scale: 1.2){x}`.
- **Structures:** scripts, fractions (stacked, slashed, diagonal), radicals with indexes, large operators with limits, fences, accents, relative rotation; TeX atom-class spacing.
- **Styles:** contexts with faces by role and parameter rules, bases; math font packages (`stix-two`, `libertinus`) as faces and rules.
- **Setting:** on straight or curved tracks, rows or columns (a character at a time, upright), broken at spaces, soft hyphens, relations and binary operators.
- **Against v1, still missing:** weight matching of faces (v1 grew a math face's strokes to the text's), spacing by ink (contact profiles), drawn arrows, math alphabets (\mathbb…), a member's coverage ranges and size matching, the Greek mapping, underline and strikethrough, MATH size variants.

### Fonts (`lib/fontsearch`, `apps/fonts`)

- Text styles with faces by role (text, italic, bold, bold italic, math, fallbacks) and rules (since 2026-11-16; composed fonts before). Math font packages (STIX Two, Libertinus, fetched by `scripts/fetch-fonts.py`) are faces and rules applied to a style; `graven hp15c stix-two` sets the keypad in one.
- Font search with the `graven-fonts` tool, by name, likeness, character coverage, style, math support, embedding permission and licence.
- The Fonts window: search, side-by-side and overlay comparison, and composition.
- Style sizes are given in points.
- **Web containers:** WOFF and WOFF2 are read wherever a font is (`lib/font/container.zig`; `Face.init` unwraps them, so indexing and labels take them too). WOFF is unwrapped table by table (zlib); WOFF2 decoded (Brotli 1.1.0's C decoder, pinned; the `glyf`/`loca` and `hmtx` transforms reversed; collections, shared tables once). `graven-fonts convert FILE... [--to FOLDER]` stores a font as a plain `.ttf`, `.otf`, `.ttc` or `.otc`, named by its outlines (never converted between kinds). Checked against fontTools on 33 fonts (system fonts and the math packages): every WOFF came back byte-identical, every WOFF2 with the same tables and all 51,786 glyphs point for point. Graven does not write WOFF or WOFF2.

### Layout model (`lib/layout`)

- **Canvas:** units of mm, inches or mils, with an optional sheet.
- **Spaces and frames:** frames are spaces of their own. Views place one space inside another.
- **Structure:** layers, images, groups, and object IDs that last.
- **Panels:** margins and compliance checks. Panels can be made from closed paths and can have cutouts.
- **Tiles:** tile prototypes with tracks on their faces (rows, or columns set a character at a time), sizing rules, art drawn at 1u and stretched, and their own unit.
- **Tracks:**
  - rows or columns along a path with control points, where segments step by pitch and are bound to the path at their centre;
  - the path and segment length define the track's boundary;
  - threaded tracks, where one flow runs through joined tracks, including across panels.
  - two kinds (2026-11-20): regions, which add rows or columns as content needs them, and tracks, one line each, laid out in advance; orientation as a baseline direction and a content rotation, rows and columns its axis cases.
- **Flows:**
  - tiles, blanks, text and equations in one stream;
  - hard breaks and paragraphs with paragraph styles (spacing, alignment, indents);
  - groups that break as one;
  - flowing around line art and tall tiles.
- **Constraints:** object origins and features, solved per axis. Constraints can apply to track path control points. Moving and resizing only use the freedom constraints leave.
- **Line art** on panels.
- **RTF import** into a flow.

### Documents and undo

- The native `.graven` format: a checkpoint codec plus an operation log. Undo history is kept in the file, so undo works after reopening.
- Autosave, crash recovery, and protection when another session has written the file.
- History limits, plus the `graven dump` and `graven compact` commands.

### GUI editor (`apps/gui`, about 12,000 lines)

- **Window and panes:** a DVUI/SDL3 app with its own title bar. The tree, tools and properties are see-through overlay panes. It matches the desktop theme.
- **Canvas:** rulers in the document's units.
- **Expression line:** brace pairing, command completion, a live preview and a cursor drawn in the rendered label.
- **Flow editing like a word processor:**
  - a character cursor moved with dvui's text-field keys, and Shift to select;
  - typing, Backspace and Delete, cut, copy and paste;
  - Enter for a new paragraph, Shift+Enter for a line break;
  - an equation edited on the canvas itself with Ctrl+\\ (typing into its source, Tab through its heads and fields, single keys on a head to style it);
  - soft-hyphen breaks and forced wraps, with faint marks while editing.
- **Selection and moving:** Ctrl-click selection with Tab to hunt for the next-nearest object. Drag to move, with handles to resize. The pointer shows a hand or a lock. Objects can be moved or copied into frames.
- **Constraint editor:** the Constrain tool, glyphs and an origin picker.
- **Background reflow:** changes that spread across pages reflow on a worker thread, in batches.
- **Settings:** shared and local TOML config, and locale detection for language and measurement system.
- **Nudges** (2026-11-17, step 4 of moving and sizing; reworked at the owner's word): the arrows move the selection, whatever was clicked, panels included, by the grid's minor unit at the zoom (its finest lines that show), Shift+arrow by its major unit (its strongest lines) (`canvas.selection.nudge`, `…-far`), and the status line says how far ("Moved 1/16 in", "Moved 10 mm"); a group moves as a whole; a lone selected track by its own step (pitch × its tiles' step; Shift: its pitch). The grid itself divides inches as a ruler does, in halves from 1/64 to the inch and twelve inches to the foot; millimetres and mils in tenths (`grid.Scale`). Through the constraints (`placeObjects`), and a run of nudges is one undo step. With nothing selected, the context (line art, an image, a view, a group) moves the same way (`canvas.object.nudge`, `…-far`), and a track segment picked in the Browser by its own step (Shift: four). Fixed on the way: the "Font … is not installed" notice was set again after every edit (fonts are re-resolved then), hiding any message the edit had just given; it now shows when it changes.
- **Track extents always shown** (2026-11-17, at the owner's request): each track region's full extent is drawn dashed whether selected or not (faint; stronger for the context's track).
- **One selection** (2026-11-17, step 3 of moving and sizing): a click selects. The context (what the properties show) is what `pick` finds, and the container it is (a panel, a track, line art, an image, a group) becomes the selection, with its box and the handles its constraints leave. Clicking again at the same place selects the container it sits in, and so on out, then back in; a tile (no container) steps out to its track, or for a grid's tile its panel. Shift-click (Ctrl-click too) adds the container there to the selection or takes it out. A drag on a selected container moves the selection; on an unselected panel, line art, image or group selects and moves it (a panel as before, stopping where it would stop complying); on empty canvas draws out an area and selects what lies wholly inside it. Space held (`view.pan-hold`, released by its key going up; unchorded, so typing keeps it) and a drag, or a middle-button drag, or the wheel, pan. A click that puts the flow cursor in a text track selects nothing. Clicking into text (2026-11-23): a press well inside a used segment of a text track (more than two edge widths, at most a quarter of its height, from its sides) puts the flow cursor there and starts typing, in Select boundaries too (where only a press near a segment's edge now goes to the boundaries); a drag from there selects text, Shift-click extends the selection, and Edit has Cut, Copy, Paste and Select all for it (the system clipboard, plain text: an equation copies as its characters, not its source).
- **The spark as the cursor** (2026-11-17; `cursor.zig`): a point standing in for the pointer (a locked engagement or sketch point, a point being dragged) is the system cursor: an animated cursor (SDL 3.4) whose image holds the spark where the point is, off from the hotspot by the point's offset (and, while only hovering, the pointer's arrow at the hotspot), 20 frames of 40 ms, a seamless 0.8 s loop, with an image at the screen's scale too. The compositor plays it: Graven draws nothing while it runs (measured, release: 0 frames in 2 s locked and still; 5 dragging and still; was 25 a second drawn). Made afresh when the offset changes; DVUI is kept from setting its own cursor over it, and gets it back when it ends. Drawn on the canvas instead where a cursor cannot be made (more than 256 px across, no animated cursors) or with `GRAVEN_DRAWN_SPARK=1`. Fixed on the way: hovering on the canvas, a lock, and sketch mode each asked for a redraw on every pointer-position event (one comes every frame), so the canvas redrew continuously (57 frames a second, release) whenever the pointer was over it; now only when something changes.
- **Engagement points** (2026-11-17; main.zig, "Engagement points"): with the select tool, every container on the canvas (panels, tracks, line art, images, views; groups by their centre) offers its corners and edge midpoints (resize two edges, one), its edges (resize one) and its centre (move). The nearest within 10 px (points) or 6 px (edges) is highlighted (a ring; an edge thickened) with an annunciator beside the pointer ("Resize HP-15C plate: bottom-right corner", "Move …", or "…: left edge held by a constraint", the lock in the warning colour). Tab locks onto it, further Tabs onto the next-nearest (Shift+Tab back; mode `canvas.engage`), Esc or going 60 px away lets go; on X11 the pointer is put on the locked point (Wayland does not allow it). A drag from it moves the point itself: it follows the pointer's motion from where it was taken, and the container's edges (or centre) go exactly there; meanwhile the system pointer is hidden and the point scintillates. A press that does not drag is a click. Policy for the whole interface: a point standing in for the pointer scintillates (a ring of turning rays in two trading colours, pulsing, round a fine cross); the sketch's locked point now does too. Still to come, as agreed: one selection for click and Ctrl-click, empty-canvas drag as a selection rectangle with Space-drag to pan, nudges for all (Shift ×10), snapping with guides, editable X/Y/W/H.
- **Pane layouts** (2026-11-17; `layouts.zig`): each pane has an official name (`browser`, the tree, now labelled Browser; `tools`; `properties`; `expression-bar`), used in the config and as its label's message ID. A layout gives each pane its mode, size (the browser's width, the right column's, the tools' height) and text scale (its fonts, and the expression bar's height with them). Built in: standard (as before), compact (side panes auto-hide, 0.9 text, narrower), large-text (1.25 text, wider). Layouts live in the shared file under `panes.layouts.<name>.<pane>`; the one in use is `panes.layout` in the local file; a pane changed by hand is written to the local file as an override of only what differs; View › Pane layout lists them all (built-ins and the config's), and has Next layout, Save layout as… (the panes as they are, into the shared file, then in use) and Reset this layout (drops this machine's overrides). Actions `panes.layout-next`, `panes.layout-save-as`, `panes.layout-reset` (no keys yet; a hot corner can run them). The old per-pane keys (`panes.tree.mode` …, `panes.tools_height`) are read once when no layout is set. Not yet: placing panes on other edges.
- **Held on one axis, free on the other** (2026-11-18, from the owner's report): as in sketches, a point goes as far as its constraints let it. An engagement point (or a selection handle) is offered if any of its directions is free, and a drag moves only those: a corner whose one edge is held resizes along the other ("Resize Tracks 1: bottom-right corner (bottom edge only; right edge held by a constraint)"), a move held across goes up and down only. Before, a corner needed both its edges free, and a move moved both axes and left the solver to pull one back.
- **The origin is a move grab point** (2026-11-18, from the owner's report): every container's origin (a track's top-left mark, ⊕) is an engagement point that moves it ("Move Tracks 1 by its origin"), ahead of a corner at the same place; Tab reaches the corner's resize. Before, a track's top-left engaged only the corner resize, and with its width constrained it said "top-left corner held by a constraint" and would not move.
- **Placing and picking dimensions** (2026-11-18, at the owner's request): where a constraint's dimension is drawn is now the constraint's own (`Constraint.place`: `across`, mm sideways from the middle of the constrained object, and `along`, how far along the line its value sits, 0 to 1), so it moves with what it constrains, is undone (`setConstraintPlace`, a drag one step) and is written with the file (old files read with the default, the line through the middle and the value halfway). With the select tool a dimension's line and its value are engagement points: hovering highlights the dimension with an annunciator ("87.5 mm: Tracks 1's left edge from its right edge · drag the line to place it, click to pick it"); dragging the line slides it sideways, dragging the value slides it along the line (and the line with it). A click picks the constraint: the properties show it (what it holds, its value, Place it as at first, Remove) and Delete removes it. Containers win a tie with a dimension at the same distance. Not yet: equal-size marks are not placeable or pickable; a dimension is not kept outside the window or clear of others by itself.
- **A stroke font for dimensions** (2026-11-18, at the owner's request; `lib/geom/strokefont.zig`): dimension values and constraint marks are drawn as strokes, not text: polylines placed by a transform (any angle, any size, y up or down), drawn each frame, nothing rasterised or cached. Glyphs: Hershey's Roman Simplex for printable ASCII (credited to Dr. A. V. Hershey, U. S. National Bureau of Standards, as its terms ask), with ° ⌀ Ø ± × − ↔ ↕ ′ ″ · ∠ drawn to match; anything else draws as `?`. `scripts/strokefont.py` writes `strokefont_data.zig` from the `.jhf` file. The canvas's dimensions put their value along their line, upright (a vertical one reads upward), and hit-test the value by its box. In a sketch, a distance's value runs along its line, and a radius has a leader from the centre through the curve (a circle at 45°, an arc at its middle), an arrowhead on the curve and its value turned along the leader beyond it; other marks (H, V, =, …) are stroke text beside their anchor. Cap height 9 logical pixels, 1 px strokes. Not measured in a release build yet (a few dozen short polylines a frame). Not yet: diameter dimensions (the sketch has radius only; ⌀ is ready), dimension text in exports.
- **Snap to grid** (2026-11-18, step 5 of moving and sizing, grid part): a drag puts what it moves on the grid's finest lines showing at the zoom (the nudge's minor unit, so the step follows the zoom and the document's units; inches by ruler fractions). What snaps: an engaged point (a container's origin, centre, corner, edge; held axes stay held), a selection handle's edges, a moved selection's first container's top-left (the rest move with it), a dragged panel's origin, and the corners of a new panel, track or line art (and polygon clicks; tracks and line art rounded to 0.5 mm before, panels to 1 mm). While dragging, a guide runs across the canvas through each grid line the point is on. Alt held lets go for as long as it is held; View › Snap to grid (`%`, Inkscape's key; `view.snap-grid`) turns it off, kept in the shared config (`view.snap_grid`); with the grid set to none nothing snaps. Not yet (rest of step 5): snapping to other containers' edges and centres; sketch points do not snap to the grid.

- **Process profiles, step 1: the model** (2026-11-19, the owner's direction: profiles under a top-level classification, FDM first, with nozzle sizes down to 0.2 mm, since 0.4 mm is expected to be too coarse for the HP-15C). `lib/layout/process.zig`: a **class** (additive, subtractive, formed, finishing) above a **process** (FDM, under additive, for now); a **profile** names one way of making: its parameters (FDM: nozzle, line width, layer height, first layer, filament slots, build volume) and its **limits**, derived from the parameters unless overridden, each a band where less is worse (pass at or above, fail below, marginal between): stroke passes at the line width and fails below half the nozzle; gap passes at the nozzle and fails below half of it; cap height passes at 3.75 nozzles and fails below 2.5 (1.5 and 1.0 mm with 0.4); inlays and cuts whole layers, two at least; colours up to the slots; the part within the build volume. **Built-ins:** Generic FDM with 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mm nozzles (line widths 0.22, 0.42, 0.62, 0.82; layers half the nozzle). The document keeps the profiles its panels use (`Model.processes`; `addProcess`, `setProcess`, `removeProcess` (refused while in use), `setPanelProcess`, undoable); a panel's `Fab.process` names one (none until chosen). Gone from the panel: layer height and the three limits; an older file's are read and become a profile on opening (`upgradeProcesses`: the built-in 0.4 mm one where they were the defaults, as in the examples, else one of their own, shared by panels alike). The layer-height flag now reads the panel's profile. The GUI shows the panel's process in Fabrication (choosing it is step 2); `graven-fab` reads the layer height from it. Next: step 3 (the checks).

- **Process profiles, step 3, first stage: the checks in the library** (2026-11-19). `lib/layout/check.zig`, `graven check [--panel] [--svg] <document>`. First built on slabs with exact booleans: minutes, far too slow (the owner: a different approach, as a slicer lays paths). Rebuilt on the owner's suggestion, as OrcaSlicer lays walls: piece by piece (glyph, hole, part), overlaps resolved, as polygons at 0.005 mm. An inward offset by half a bead and a growth back leaves uncovered what is too thin: marginal below the line width, fail below the narrowest bead; closing finds counters and notches that would close; necks between marks on a part, a mark near its part's edge, parts too close. Faces losing only their corners (computed from the polygon) skip the cut; results cached by shape. Cap height, depths, colours, size alongside. Found and fixed on the way: `fill.nonzero` failed to join where a crossing lay near a vertex's height on near-horizontal edges (the top of a curve's offset), so a growth came back in pieces; edges are now split at crossings through a shared point (a test from a real S). `fill`'s crossing search and slabs now sweep. Measured (release, HP-15C): keys 0.94 s cold, 0.17 s warm; plate 0.60 s cold, 0.13 s warm. The HP-15C at 0.2 mm: the large legends pass; the small blue and gold legends (cap about 1.3 mm) are marginal across their horizontals, and superscripts fail. The owner: plenty fast; the checks may be wholly manual.

- **Manufacturing plans, step 1: the model** (2026-11-19; blueprint "Manufacturing plans and views"). `lib/layout/manufacture.zig`:
  - **Materials:** a kind, a stock form, a surface (bare, anodised, coated, two-colour laminate) and its core, finishes, sheet thicknesses. A built-in library: PLA and PETG filament, cast acrylic, black-over-white engraving laminate, black anodised aluminium, stainless steel, birch plywood.
  - **What a process can do with a material** (`fit`: good, poor or no, with why) and the colours it can give there (`results`):
    - laser on anodising: the bare metal;
    - laser on a laminate: its core;
    - laser on wood: char;
    - FDM: the filaments;
    - cutting: none of its own.
  - **Plans:** span panels (their parts, each with a material) and hold operations in free order. Each operation is a process profile over stackup layers of any part, by name, with a colour for each.
  - **Warnings, never refusals:** no material, unsuited, colour unavailable, marking after a cut, no such target, unmade layer, too thick, no process.

  `process.zig` gains laser cutting, laser engraving and marking, and waterjet. Their limits come from kerf or spot; built-ins: CO2 laser cutter, laser engraver, fibre marker, waterjet. In the model, materials, plans and the active plan are undoable (add, set, remove), saved, and kept out of removal while in use. Older documents gain a plan on opening: their panels of PLA, with one print operation per profile in the layers' colours. The active plan's colours feed the fabrication plan (the layer's own where it gives none); the HP-15C's 3MF and the keyboard SVG are unchanged. The GUI shows the new kinds' settings in a profile's properties.
- **Manufacturing plans, step 2: the manufacturing view and the plan's editor** (2026-11-19).

What an operation adds is filled in the colour it gives, else the part's material's; what it cuts away shows the backdrop, its edge drawn. Cutting is drawn first, as the stock it shapes, then the rest in the plan's order. The wheel zooms, a drag pans; it opens fitted, and its look is kept with the document's views.
  - **The Browser:** Manufacturing plans (always listed; Add a plan, over every panel with fabrication layers) and Materials (Add from the library).
  - **A plan's editor,** in its properties and whenever a manufacturing view is active:

    - Parts: each panel's material, from the document's or the library's (added on choosing), and Add a part;
    - Operations: Add an operation; each one shown or hidden in the view, Earlier, Later, Remove, its name, process (the document's profiles or a built-in, added on choosing) and passes, and the parts' stackup layers it makes (checkboxes), each with a colour from what that process gives on that part's material (a layer's own colour where the process gives it, else the process's first, as a laser's mark);

- **Manufacturing plans, step 3: checks and simulation per operation** (2026-11-19). `check.runWith(…, Scope)` checks one operation:
  - **Scope:** against its own process profile, on only the stackup layers it makes, their cap heights and depths. Colours are counted only for additive processes. Paths take the colour the operation gives.
  - **Simulation by process:** FDM's walls (two in a part, inlays filled, two round a hole); a laser cutter's or waterjet's kerf path, once, outside a part's edge and inside its holes, in the kerf colour; an engraver's passes, a spot apart, until the mark is filled. Cached by shape, process and width. `check.run` is unchanged for a panel against its own profile.
  - **In the GUI:** Check this plan (or C in a manufacturing view) runs every operation on each part it works on. The results draw in the plan's view under their operation's layer: its simulated paths, then its findings, with Findings and Simulation shown or hidden, and Clear results. Each operation shows its counts in the editor, and the results are marked out of date after an edit.
  - **On the HP-15C,** the aluminium plan in 300 ms: the waterjet's kerf inside the 39 holes and round the plate, the webs between neighbouring holes (3.43 mm) marginal against four kerfs; the engraving's math legends failing at a 0.1 mm spot.
- **Manufacturing plans: the 3D view through OCCT** (2026-11-19; blueprint "Manufacturing visualization through OCCT", route 1: OCCT models and meshes, Graven draws).
  - **The shim:** `os_solid_mesh`, OCCT's BRepMesh, each face's triangles wound outward; tested on a block (12 triangles, all facing out).
  - **`bridge/occt/view3d.zig`:** a plan's part slabs as solids (the bridge's `fab.build`), meshed, offset to where the part sits. The GUI imports it when built with `-Docct-prebuilt` (or `-Docct=`); without OCCT it imports `apps/gui/occt3d_stub.zig`, which says it is not in the build (the harness uses the stub).
  - **In the GUI:** a manufacturing view's "Shown as: From above / 3D, through OCCT" (in its plan's properties).
    - The parts are built as the plan makes them: only the layers its operations make, each in the colour it gives, else the material's.
    - The 2D slabs are made on the GUI thread (about 0.2 s); OCCT's sweeping and meshing run in a background thread ("building it through OCCT…"), about 6 s for the HP-15C (405,704 triangles, mostly OCCT's meshing).
    - Drawing: triangles facing the camera, in an order exact for prisms seen from above (up through the heights; at each, walls farthest first, then tops), shaded by facing. A drag turns, Shift-drag moves, the wheel zooms about the pointer.
    - About 45 ms a frame while turning the HP-15C.
    - First tried by depth alone: thin inlaid strokes were broken up by walls drawn over them; hence the order by height.

## Display: MSDF glyphs (in progress)

`geom.msdf` makes a glyph's multi-channel signed distance field: overlaps resolved first, corners found and edges coloured, signed pseudo-distances per channel, signs checked against the true inside. Drawn back at any size its area is within 1 % of the outline's, corners stay sharp, and growing is a shifted threshold (the outline grown with round corners, as `geom.grow`). About 1.5 ms a glyph at 32 texels per cap height, made once per glyph. `graven msdf` draws a sheet of glyphs at several sizes, plain and grown. The GUI draws text from it (`apps/gui/glyphs.zig`): a field per face and glyph, sampled into coverage rasters by quarter octave of size and eighth of a pixel of growth, packed into 1024 px atlas pages (8, then the atlas starts again), each glyph a textured quad with its own transform; rules and drawn marks stay outlines, as does everything while construction is shown or when a glyph would raster past 256 px. Upright glyphs are rastered on the pixel grid at their exact size (64ths of an octave, horizontal phase in quarter pixels, baseline and cap height on pixel rows) and drawn texel for pixel; coverage takes a gamma of 2.2 (stem darkening, up to a set number of pixels fading out by 32 px cap height, is off by default) (`GRAVEN_TEXT_GAMMA`, `GRAVEN_TEXT_DARKEN`). `GRAVEN_EXACT_TEXT=1` draws text from its outlines instead, to compare; `GRAVEN_SAMPLE=hp15c|keyboard` and `GRAVEN_VIEW=cx,cy,ppm` open the app on a sample at a view. Next: the error-correction pass (faint specks where grown strokes merge), fields made off the drawing thread, least-recently-used eviction.

Fields come at three resolutions, chosen by the size a glyph is drawn: 32 texels per cap height up to a 64 px cap, 64 to 128 px, 128 past that (at least a texel for every two pixels; the range doubles with them, staying an eighth of the cap height). A gap a grown stroke nearly closes, drawn broken from a coarse field, is whole at the finer ones and matches the exact outline. Glyphs off the canvas are skipped before any field or raster is made. Release-build field times: about 4, 20 and 100 ms a glyph at the three levels.

Fields are made off the drawing thread, by up to 4 worker threads (one fewer than the processors): the drawing thread asks for a field, keeps drawing the glyph from the finest coarser field it has (or the label from its outlines), and takes the finished fields in at the start of a frame; the workers wake it when some are done. Release build, HP-15C keypad, worst frame on opening: 139 to 205 ms with fields made inline, 22 to 56 ms now. `GRAVEN_FIELDS_INLINE=1` makes them inline, to compare.
