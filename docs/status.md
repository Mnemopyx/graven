# Graven status

Taken on 13 November 2026, on branch `ui-dev`. This list comes from the commit history, the code layout and recent work. Not every feature was re-checked in the app for it.

Graven has about 32,000 lines of Zig and 209 tests. It has 127 commits since 4 November 2026, and 81 of them are on `ui-dev` and not yet on `main`.

## What exists

### Label engine (`lib/engine`, `lib/font`, `lib/geom`)

- **Shaping and outlines:** HarfBuzz shaping. Glyph outlines are kept exact (lines, quadratics and cubics), and cap height is measured from the outlines.
- **Markup:** KaTeX-style label markup with a strict parse and a lenient preview parse. Text in a track with `$…$` is text with math between the dollars, as in KaTeX: math's letters are variables (italic; from a math font package's face for variables), words in it `\mathrm`; scripts belong in math. Track text without dollars is all one markup, as before.
- **Math font packages:** `stix-two` (Geist SemiBold text, STIX Two Math for constants and symbols, STIX Two Text Bold Greek, TeX Gyre Schola Bold Italic variables), fetched by `scripts/fetch-fonts.py`; `graven hp15c stix-two …` sets the keypad in it.
- **Member weight:** a composed font's member can be drawn heavier: `.match` (its stem of I, relative to cap height as drawn, grown to the main face's), a stroke increase in thousandths of cap height, or none. Its glyphs are grown outward by half the increase all round (`geom.grow`: the region's Minkowski sum with a disc, convex corners rounded, counters shrinking) and spaced by it. The STIX Two package matches STIX Two Math's symbols to Geist SemiBold (0.138 to 0.183 of cap height).
- **Libertinus package:** `libertinus` (chosen for the HP-15C keypad): Geist SemiBold text; Libertinus Math 7.051 for operators and arrows (+ < = > ± × ÷ −, the Arrows and Mathematical Operators blocks), bold Greek from the mathematical bold block (a composed font's `greek` setting maps Δ Σ π to U+1D6A8..), math-italic variables (U+1D434..; with no member for variables, math's letters become those), and the MATH constants. `graven hp15c libertinus …`.
- **Matching by stroke:** a member's weight match names a reference stroke: an I's stem for letters, the minus sign's bar for operators (math fonts draw operators thinner than their letters), the member's own bold or italic I for those letters. Growth follows the member that set each character, so one face can carry several. Bold Greek is left as drawn: growing it fills the acute corners where Libertinus's hairlines meet its thick strokes.
- **Drawn arrows:** a style's `arrows` draws → ← ↑ ↓ and ⇄ instead of taking them from a face, as on the HP keypad: a solid triangular head pointed well under a right angle (0.45 cap long, 0.5 wide), a short shaft as heavy as the text's stem of I. ⇄ is two heads alone, right over left, each notched at the back where a shaft would go in. The HP-15C sample turns them on.
  - Superscripts and subscripts, `\frac`, `\sqrt`, accents and math spaces.
  - TeX atom-class spacing.
  - Variants: bold, italic and math alphabets.
  - Underline, strikethrough, and text superscripts and subscripts.
- **Spacing by ink:** neighbours and scripts are placed by their ink, using contact profiles.
- **Line breaking:** breaks within a box, at spaces, `\allowbreak` and soft hyphens, and forced cuts inside words that are too long for their line.
- **Spring-chain solver** for anchoring labels.

### Fonts (`lib/fontsearch`, `apps/fonts`)

- Text styles with faces by role (text, italic, bold, bold italic, math, fallbacks) and rules (since 2026-11-16; composed fonts before). Math font packages (STIX Two, Libertinus, fetched by `scripts/fetch-fonts.py`) are faces and rules applied to a style; `graven hp15c stix-two` sets the keypad in one.
- Font search with the `graven-fonts` tool, by name, likeness, character coverage, style, math support, embedding permission and licence.
- The Fonts window: search, side-by-side and overlay comparison, and composition.
- Style sizes are given in points.
- **Web containers:** WOFF and WOFF2 are read wherever a font is (`lib/font/container.zig`; `Face.init` unwraps them, so indexing and labels take them too). WOFF is unwrapped table by table (zlib); WOFF2 decoded (Brotli 1.1.0's C decoder, pinned; the `glyf`/`loca` and `hmtx` transforms reversed; collections, shared tables once). `graven-fonts convert FILE... [--to FOLDER]` stores a font as a plain `.ttf`, `.otf`, `.ttc` or `.otc`, named by its outlines (never converted between kinds). Checked against fontTools on 33 fonts (system fonts and the math packages): every WOFF came back byte-identical, every WOFF2 with the same tables and all 51,786 glyphs point for point. Graven does not write WOFF or WOFF2.

### Layout model (`lib/layout`)

- **Canvas:** units of mm, inches or mils, with an optional sheet.
- **Spaces and frames:** frames are spaces of their own. Views place one space inside another.
- **Structure:** layers, images, groups, and object IDs that last.
- **Panels:** margins and compliance checks. Panels can be made from closed paths and can have cutouts.
- **Tiles:** tile prototypes with tracks on their faces (rows, or columns set a character at a time), sizing rules, art drawn at 1u and stretched, and their own unit.
- **Tracks:**
  - rows or columns along a path with control points, where segments step by pitch and are bound to the path at their centre;
  - the path and segment length define the track's boundary;
  - threaded tracks, where one flow runs through joined tracks, including across panels.
- **Flows:**
  - tiles, blanks, text and equations in one stream;
  - hard breaks and paragraphs with paragraph styles (spacing, alignment, indents);
  - groups that break as one;
  - flowing around line art and tall tiles.
- **Constraints:** object origins and features, solved per axis. Constraints can apply to track path control points. Moving and resizing only use the freedom constraints leave.
- **Line art** on panels.
- **RTF import** into a flow.

### Documents and undo

- The native `.graven` format: a checkpoint codec plus an operation log. Undo history is kept in the file, so undo works after reopening.
- Autosave, crash recovery, and protection when another session has written the file.
- History limits, plus the `graven dump` and `graven compact` commands.

### GUI editor (`apps/gui`, about 12,000 lines)

- **Window and panes:** a DVUI/SDL3 app with its own title bar. The tree, tools and properties are see-through overlay panes. It matches the desktop theme.
- **Canvas:** rulers in the document's units.
- **Expression line:** brace pairing, command completion, a live preview and a cursor drawn in the rendered label.
- **Flow editing like a word processor:**
  - a character cursor moved with dvui's text-field keys, and Shift to select;
  - typing, Backspace and Delete, cut, copy and paste;
  - Enter for a new paragraph, Shift+Enter for a line break;
  - equation mode with Ctrl+\\, and Tab through an equation's slots;
  - soft-hyphen breaks and forced wraps, with faint marks while editing.
- **Selection and moving:** Ctrl-click selection with Tab to hunt for the next-nearest object. Drag to move, with handles to resize. The pointer shows a hand or a lock. Objects can be moved or copied into frames.
- **Constraint editor:** the Constrain tool, glyphs and an origin picker.
- **Background reflow:** changes that spread across pages reflow on a worker thread, in batches.
- **Settings:** shared and local TOML config, and locale detection for language and measurement system.

### Output and command line

- SVG export from the GUI, and from the `graven` CLI: `svg`, `label`, `parse`, `keyboard`, `rtf`, `dump`, `compact`.
- The PC keyboard sample. Its output is the regression check run after each change.
- Colour 3D printing: a part's stack of layer operations (Panel.fab: add, subtract, inlay; from the base's surfaces, up or down) is resolved into slabs of exact 2D colour regions (layout.fab), then written as 3MF (`graven 3mf hp15c`, under a second for both HP-15C parts: closed meshes, one per colour (the union of its bands) or per colour and band, standing on the part's bottom or turned over onto its top; earcut faces, colours as materials) or, through OCCT, as STEP (`graven-fab`, slower: exact text is about a million STEP entities per part). Not yet: Manufacture mode in the GUI, manufacturing rules beyond the layer-height flag, slicer-specific filament assignment.

## Display: MSDF glyphs (in progress)

`geom.msdf` makes a glyph's multi-channel signed distance field: overlaps resolved first, corners found and edges coloured, signed pseudo-distances per channel, signs checked against the true inside. Drawn back at any size its area is within 1 % of the outline's, corners stay sharp, and growing is a shifted threshold (the outline grown with round corners, as `geom.grow`). About 1.5 ms a glyph at 32 texels per cap height, made once per glyph. `graven msdf` draws a sheet of glyphs at several sizes, plain and grown. The GUI draws text from it (`apps/gui/glyphs.zig`): a field per face and glyph, sampled into coverage rasters by quarter octave of size and eighth of a pixel of growth, packed into 1024 px atlas pages (8, then the atlas starts again), each glyph a textured quad with its own transform; rules and drawn marks stay outlines, as does everything while construction is shown or when a glyph would raster past 256 px. Upright glyphs are rastered on the pixel grid at their exact size (64ths of an octave, horizontal phase in quarter pixels, baseline and cap height on pixel rows) and drawn texel for pixel; coverage takes a gamma of 2.2 (stem darkening, up to a set number of pixels fading out by 32 px cap height, is off by default) (`GRAVEN_TEXT_GAMMA`, `GRAVEN_TEXT_DARKEN`). `GRAVEN_EXACT_TEXT=1` draws text from its outlines instead, to compare; `GRAVEN_SAMPLE=hp15c|keyboard` and `GRAVEN_VIEW=cx,cy,ppm` open the app on a sample at a view. Next: the error-correction pass (faint specks where grown strokes merge), fields made off the drawing thread, least-recently-used eviction.

Fields come at three resolutions, chosen by the size a glyph is drawn: 32 texels per cap height up to a 64 px cap, 64 to 128 px, 128 past that (at least a texel for every two pixels; the range doubles with them, staying an eighth of the cap height). A gap a grown stroke nearly closes, drawn broken from a coarse field, is whole at the finer ones and matches the exact outline. Glyphs off the canvas are skipped before any field or raster is made. Release-build field times: about 4, 20 and 100 ms a glyph at the three levels.

Fields are made off the drawing thread, by up to 4 worker threads (one fewer than the processors): the drawing thread asks for a field, keeps drawing the glyph from the finest coarser field it has (or the label from its outlines), and takes the finished fields in at the start of a frame; the workers wake it when some are done. Release build, HP-15C keypad, worst frame on opening: 139 to 205 ms with fields made inline, 22 to 56 ms now. `GRAVEN_FIELDS_INLINE=1` makes them inline, to compare.

## Font management (stages 1, 2 and the local part of 5 built)

Decided 2026-11-15, revised 2026-11-16 to the owner's model (blueprint, "Font management"): system fonts and user fonts are scanned into one index, each face marked system or user, and the local interface groups faces by typeface family. Font files are installed as user fonts, in the OS's per-user font folder where every application finds them (no separate Graven library); system fonts are never written. Remote search and fetch go through plugins or access methods on URLs, and fetched fonts may be installed for the user. Everything installed is plain sfnt, with a provenance record in Graven's data folder.

- **Built, stage 1 (containers):** WOFF/WOFF2 to sfnt, `graven-fonts convert`.
- **Built, stage 2 (local fonts):** `Record.scope` (system, user, remote; index version 6); `fontsearch.families` (faces by typographic family, ordered by width, weight and slope, counted by scope); the local source scans the user's folder (Linux `$XDG_DATA_HOME/fonts` or `~/.local/share/fonts`, and `~/.fonts`; macOS `~/Library/Fonts`; Windows `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\Windows\Fonts`) as user fonts. `graven-fonts families [words] [--user|--system]`, `install FILE... [--replace]` (converted, named Family-Style.ext, a same-content repeat does nothing, a different user font of the same family and style kept unless `--replace`, a system font of the same name noted), `remove "Family" [--style S]` (user fonts only); `show` gives scope and provenance (`installed-fonts.json` in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/graven`, `~/Library/Application Support/Graven` or `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Graven`: SHA-256 of the file and of what came in, from, container, date, family, style, licence as stated).
- **Built, the Fonts window (local part of stage 5):** results grouped by family (families in the order of their best-ranked face; a family's heading opens and closes it, open by default when the results have 60 faces or fewer, and says how many faces in how many files and whether system, user or both). An open family shows its installed files, and under each file the weights and styles it holds: every face in it, by width, weight and slope (those the search left out shown faintly, so a file always shows all it carries); a variable font file its weight and width ranges and named styles; a collection its count. Remove sits on the file. **Install** (the toolbar's download icon): font files or folders, one per line, or Browse (the native dialog, where there is one); "Install over a user font of the same family and style"; a report line per file. **Remove** (a user file's bin icon) removes the file, so it first says what goes with it: every face the file holds (a collection's faces; a variable font's weight and width ranges and named styles), what stays of each family, and which of the document's styles use the faces going (by role) and whether another face of that name takes over or the text falls back. Installs and removals rescan the index; the selection, the likeness reference and pins stay where their fonts still are.
- **Fixed with it:** a rescan (the window's rescan button too, before this) freed faces the canvas's resolved fonts still held. The index now has a generation; the editor resolves its fonts again (and places again) when it changes, and faces of earlier generations are freed only after that.
- **Variable fonts** are named `Family[axes].ext` when installed ("Geist[wght].ttf"); a collection's install reports all its faces. `Face.axis`, `axisTags` and `namedInstances` read fvar.
- **Built, the Fonts window layout:** everything visible at once. The search criteria form a strip across the top (Search, Style, Licence terms); results and Compare sit side by side below, Style faces (Compose until 2026-11-16) to the right. A family is one line: style icons for Regular, Bold, Italic and Bold Italic (the letter a drawn in that face, from its outlines; empty where the family has none; regular is the upright face nearest 400 within 300 to 500, bold the one nearest 700 at 600 or more), each slant's pair followed by its weights in small boxes (VF for variable), then the face and file count. Italic groups align in a column after the widest upright group that leaves room for them; a family too wide wraps its italic group under the upright one. An open family shows one line per file: pin, each face's icon and weight box, the file name, what it is, and Remove for a user font. Icons, boxes and file lines select a face; a family line elsewhere opens or closes it. Off-screen icons are not drawn.
- **Not yet:** Windows per-user fonts also need a registry entry (HKCU `...\Windows NT\CurrentVersion\Fonts`) for applications to see them; not written, and not tested on Windows or macOS. The canvas's missing-font status does not yet offer the Fonts window.
- **Next stages:** 3 remote search and fetch (plugins, access methods on URLs, catalogue cache, unmeasured records); 4 repositories (Fontsource, Google Fonts, GitHub releases, CTAN, URL manifests); 5 the Fonts window's remote part (remote sources, Get, provenance shown).

## Engine v2 (`lib/glyphic`): stages 1 to 4 built

The object model, beside v1 and independent of it (`graven_glyphic`, tests only). **Objects** (`expr.zig`): stable ids never reused (overrides and tags travel with an object through moves); kinds (row; number, variable, constant, symbol, operator, relation, punct, word, ellipsis; fraction, scripts, large operator, radical, fence, accent, group), an operator's role (unary, binary), named slots holding rows, required slots made as holes and optional ones opened on demand, a variable's shared symbol name, tags (source command, atom class, syntax). **Parameters** (`param.zig`): 37 typed parameters (rotation relative to the parent's frame among them: `rotate`, `rotate_pivot`, `rotate_space`; the ground's path transform is separate) (number with unit and range, choice, flag, name), each with a description and a default. **Cascade** (`cascade.zig`): font default, package, style, legend, object, each over the last; within a level, rules by kind, role, script level, symbol or slot, most specific first; each value says which level and rule set it. **Context**: math style, cramped and script level derived from structure (TeX's rules), never stored. **Stops** (`stops.zig`): heads before fields in field order, rows holding a lone structure not stops of their own, hidden and absent optional slots skipped; optional slots toggled from the head and kept while hidden. **Breaks** (`breaks.zig`): candidates on the top row only (after relations at 500, binary operators at 700; never before punctuation or ⋯), break marks forbid, allow, prefer, force. The Leibniz series is built in `samples.zig`, and the tests check each decision on it.

**Stage 2 built: the ground.** `glyphic.ground`: what layout asks as it goes, a cursor of pieces (each made on request, a paragraph's spacing passed as it is made): a baseline path with a frame at every distance along it (point, direction, up, signed curvature; straight lines and circular arcs), the open stretches, room above and below, the cap height, alignment; `fit` and `openSpans`. Process limits are not part of it (the owner, 2026-11-16: a manufacture-stage concept, nothing limited early). Synthetic grounds: `Rows` and `Dial` (concentric arcs). `lib/layout/ground.zig`'s `TrackGround` gives it for a panel's tracks from the flow pass's own parts (`Path`, `KeepOut`, `lineIn`, `baselineIn`, now `pub`), so its pieces are v1's segments band for band (tested), with art's keep-outs, a thread's next track, and `more` when the thread goes on to another panel; columns run down with up to the right. Process limits: `Fab.min_stroke`, `min_gap` (0.4 mm) and `min_cap` (1.0 mm), validated, kept in files, for the manufacture stage's checks. Relative rotation stays glyphic's; the ground has none. 17 glyphic tests, 5 ground tests in layout; keyboard and HP-15C output unchanged.

**Stage 3 built in large part: layout and output.** `faces.zig`: named faces (text, math, italic…); an object's face by its `face` parameter or its kind (italic variables from the italic face, else the math face's Mathematical Italic, else slanted), fallback through the others, missing characters reported. `layout.zig`: objects into boxes in flat expression space (mm, from the ground's cap height; script levels and `scale` size them): runs shaped (HarfBuzz, `Face.shape`), weight as growth, slant; TeX's spacing table by atom class (thin, medium, thick spaces as parameters; scripts keep only thin); fractions stacked on the math axis (MATH table, else 0.36 cap), slashed (parts at their own size, the context knowing it) or diagonal; scripts; large operators at display size, limits stacked or beside; radicals (sign scaled to the radicand, bar, index in the crook); fences grown about the axis; accents; groups; holes while editing; relative rotation about the pivot, with or without room. `setting.zig`: the top row broken into lines (of the breaks that fit, the least cost plus 1000 × unused fraction², so a relation's break wins over an operator's further on; forced breaks honoured; overflow flagged), each line aligned in its open stretch, continuations under the first relation (or indented, or as the segment aligns; back to the start when that does not fit), every glyph and rule stood on the baseline path at its place; exact outlines out (glyphs grown by their weight). `graven v2 <text font> <math font> <out.svg> [italic font]` sets the Leibniz series on a row (display style), narrow rows it breaks across, and a dial. 7 layout tests on the test fonts.

**Stage 4 built: entry syntaxes.** `latex.zig` and `typst.zig` read and write math and legends (text with math between dollars), sharing `symbols.zig` (one table: character, LaTeX command, Typst name and shorthand, kind) and `entry.zig` (spans, script attachment, unary or binary signs, tree comparison). Parsers never fail: every prefix is a whole tree, missing arguments holes, unclosed groups closed, a half-typed command upright text. LaTeX: plain `( )` are natural-size fences (scripts attach to the group), `\left…\right` grow, `\mathrm` words, `\limits`; Typst: `/` takes the unit before and after, parentheses around a fraction's part or a script's argument do not show, unknown names are upright text, functions `frac sqrt root hat abs attach upright`… Source spans per object; `reparse.carry` matches a reparsed tree to the old one (LCS per row) so overrides stay and ids follow. Tests: both syntaxes make the hand-built Leibniz tree; each writes back what it read exactly; the two translate both ways; every prefix parses; holes; spans; 20 HP-15C legends read and written back exactly and through Typst to the same objects; identity across a reparse.

**Stage 3 finished: the HP-15C through v2 against v1.** `graven v2 hp15c <folder>` lays out the keypad with v1 (libertinus package) and writes it twice: as v1 sets it, and with each of its 110 legends parsed from its markup and set by v2 in its track's band at its style's cap height (column tracks too, since 2026-11-16: ENTER). Every legend parses, no character is missing, every one fits.

**Column tracks.** A ground piece may be `stacked` (the track adapter marks columns so; `ground.Columns` is the synthetic one): its content is set a character at a time down it, each upright (standing across the baseline, its up back along it, so a curved column stands its characters across the path too) and centred on it, its capitals a quarter cap below its step's start, the next a step (`column_step`, 1.5 cap) further down and a space `column_space` (0.5 cap), as v1 sets columns. Words, numbers and letters go a character at a time (`Layout.layEach`); a structure (a fraction, scripts) stays whole and takes a longer step when tall. A full column goes on into the next piece; a space there is dropped. Tests: characters upright, centred and stepped; a structure whole; on into the next column. The HP-15C's ENTER is set by v2 now, matching v1. They agree in place, size and script position; v2 differs where v1 has features v2 does not yet: **weight matching** (v1 grows the math face's variables, √ and minus to Geist's stroke; v2's math is lighter), **spacing by ink** (v2's metric spaces around relations are a little looser), **drawn arrows** (v1 draws the HP arrows), the package's **bold Greek**. `panelSvg` gained `labels` and `extra` options for it (defaults unchanged).

**Style contexts (decided and built 2026-11-16):** no styles in the library; style configuration is part of glyphic's object system. Apps configure style contexts in `glyphic.Engine` (name, base context, faces by role, rules general or for kinds of objects) and apply them to elements, the expression, or groups outside it (given to the resolver). Resolution: an element's override where `param.Def.element` allows it (relative scale, nudges, rotation, structural choices), else the contexts reaching it innermost first, each before its base, else the default; faces likewise. Replaces the package/style/legend/object levels. The CLI's v2 commands configure their contexts; output unchanged.

**Straight onto v2 (decided 2026-11-16):** no migration from v1 (no per-style switch, no bridge, no conversion of old documents). Route: 1 text breaking in glyphic (built, below); 2 placement on v2 (built, below); 3 the model's styles become glyphic style contexts (built, below); 4 GUI on v2 (built, below); 5 CLI and samples on v2, a new keyboard baseline, `lib/engine` deleted.

**Step 1 built: text breaks in glyphic.** Legend text becomes words, spaces (kind `space`, a run of them one object) and soft hyphens (kind `soft_hyphen`; LaTeX `\-`, Typst `-?`); `\allowbreak` sets the next object's break mark to allow. Breaks at a space (`space_break_cost`, 50), the space dropped so no line starts or ends with one; at a soft hyphen (`hyphen_break_cost`, 300), the line ending with a hyphen; in columns a space takes `column_space`. A text continuation starts at the line's start (`continuation: relation` lines up under a relation only when there is one). Tests: round trips in both syntaxes; words broken across rows; a hyphenated break.

**Step 2 built: placement on v2.** Every legend on the canvas, in export and in DRC is now set by glyphic. `lib/layout/legend.zig` parses a run's markup as a legend, lays out its top row on one line and hands back a `Render`: the same container of placed glyphs and rules the flow pass, output, 3MF, STEP, DRC, the reflow worker and the GUI (atlas, carets) already read, so none of them changed. Each top-row object is one element with its source span; glyphs carry their source bytes (glyphic records each glyph's cluster); break opportunities come from `glyphic.breaks` (spaces, soft hyphens, relations and binary operators); only a text leaf's glyphs may be cut apart for a forced wrap. `Labels` keeps its cache and sketch solving; its style contexts are glyphic contexts made from the model's styles (`legend.contextOf`: upright as "text", italic, bold, bold italic, the math face, members as fallbacks; variables italic; a member scaling the math italic letters or small Greek scales variables or constants), remade when a style changes, until step 3 puts glyphic contexts in the model. Glyphic gained on the way: a node's `Variant` (bold, italic: content, chosen as the "bold"/"bold_italic" members), `\textbf`, `\textit`, `\emph`, `\textrm` and bare groups in legend text and `\mathbf`/`\boldsymbol` in math, written back out; `\textbackslash`; a text object's own source span; a missing character drawn as the first face's .notdef.
